SLIDE-TITLE = Footprint and Handprint
NARRATION-0 = Milica reads about two related ideas. One says how much we take. The other says how much we give back. She thinks: the second one is something a school can do.
CONCEPT-A = Footprint
CONCEPT-B = Handprint
CRITERION-1 = Asks
A-1 = how much nature do we use?
B-1 = what do we actively do to help?
CRITERION-2 = Side
A-2 = what you take
B-2 = what you give back
CRITERION-3 = Figure or example
A-3 = humanity uses resources as if we lived on **1.71 Earths**; the Global North uses far more than developing countries
B-3 = an action by a person or institution
CRITERION-4 = Critique or strength
A-4 = can shift responsibility to individuals instead of companies and governments
B-4 = can be started by an institution, here and now
NARRATION-1 = Here is one example of each at a school.
EXAMPLE-A = Measuring the energy and waste of the school
EXAMPLE-B = Waste sorting and energy-saving routines in workshops, with students involved
NARRATION-2 = There is one mistake to avoid.
MISCONCEPTION = "The handprint is the same as the ecological footprint." The footprint measures how much you use; the handprint stands for positive impact.
NARRATION-3 = Now you sort them.
STATEMENT-1 = Humanity uses resources as if we lived on 1.71 Earths.
STATEMENT-2 = A teacher workshop that links each subject to one boundary.
STATEMENT-3 = It can shift responsibility to individuals.
STATEMENT-4 = A new occupational profile that includes a sustainability module.
CLASSIFY-HINT = A footprint says how much you take. A handprint says how much you give back.
CLASSIFY-FEEDBACK = The footprint measures use, and has been criticised for shifting responsibility to individuals. The handprint is what you actively do. The best handprint is something an institution can actually start, not a slogan.
