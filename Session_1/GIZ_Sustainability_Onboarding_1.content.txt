NARRATION-1 = One teacher asks: so sustainability is simply a scientific term? Milica has an answer from the sustainability researcher Juliana Hilf.
QUOTE = The concept of sustainable development is not the result of scientific research; instead, it is an ethically grounded concept.
QUOTE-SOURCE = Juliana Hilf (2024), Introduction to the Sustainability Approach
NARRATION-2 = In plain words, it is about what we owe to the environment, to people today and to people who will come after us. So what is fact, and what is a goal?
PROBLEM = Science describes what is happening. The ethical question is what we owe to the environment, to people today and to people who will come after us. To work with the term, you need to keep the two apart.
POE-TITLE = One Hot Year, One Long-Term Goal
NARRATION-POE-0 = Here is a test. Stefan, the school developer, reads three statements in the news. Which one is correct?

PREDICT-WRONG-1 = The Paris goal has already been missed, because one year was above 1.5 °C.
PREDICT-WRONG-2 = The IPCC recommends political action to governments.
PREDICT-HINT = Think about how the Paris goal counts temperature: one single year, or an average over many years?
NARRATION-POE-1 = The Paris goal refers to an average over at least twenty years. And the IPCC deliberately does not recommend political action.
OBSERVATION = The Paris goal refers to an average over at least 20 years, so one year above 1.5 °C does not yet mean the goal has been missed. The IPCC provides the scientific basis for decisions; it deliberately does not recommend political action.
