[Concept_Explanation_Template_2.md]
SLIDE-TITLE = Is and Should
NARRATION-0 = At the staff meeting, a teacher asks Milica: so who has the correct definition? Her answer: nobody. Sustainability is a normative concept. This table shows what that means.
CONCEPT-A = Descriptive statement
CONCEPT-B = Normative concept
CRITERION-1 = Says
A-1 = what is
B-1 = how society *should* develop
CRITERION-2 = Basis
A-2 = measurement and evidence
B-2 = ethics, social and political guidelines
CRITERION-3 = Disagreement
A-3 = can be settled with better data
B-3 = contested: scientific and political controversies, no single "correct" definition
CRITERION-4 = Reference point
A-4 = indicators and measuring methods
B-4 = the Brundtland definition (1987), applied through models and policies
NARRATION-1 = Here are two examples.
EXAMPLE-A = "Global warming today is about 1.1 °C."
EXAMPLE-B = "Future generations must be able to meet their own needs."
NARRATION-2 = One misunderstanding comes up again and again. Sustainability is not only about the environment.
MISCONCEPTION = "Sustainability only concerns environmental protection." It also covers the economy, population development and social justice.
NARRATION-3 = Now you decide. Which concept does each statement fit?
STATEMENT-1 = 2024 was the first calendar year about 1.60 °C above the pre-industrial level.
STATEMENT-2 = Sustainability is a guideline about how society should develop.
STATEMENT-3 = The IPCC assesses the scientific evidence on climate change.
STATEMENT-4 = Sustainability is contested because people weigh values differently.
CLASSIFY-HINT = Ask yourself: does the statement report what is, or say what should be?
CLASSIFY-FEEDBACK = Statements 1 and 3 describe evidence and how it is assessed. Statements 2 and 4 are about the normative character of sustainability.
