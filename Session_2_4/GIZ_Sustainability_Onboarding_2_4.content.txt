SLIDE-TITLE = The Paris Agreement in Five Points
NARRATION-0 = Jelena writes down the five elements that hold the Paris Agreement together. Its main goal is to limit global warming to well below two degrees, preferably one point five degrees, above pre-industrial levels.
IMAGE-ALT = Plenary hall of the COP21 climate conference in Paris, 2015
IMAGE-PATH = img/1280px-Kerry_Mxakato-Diseko_Zhenhua_in_the_Plenary_Hall_at_the_COP21.jpg
IMAGE-CAPTION = COP21 plenary hall, Paris 2015 – U.S. Department of State – public domain
INTRO-TEXT = """
**Main goal:** limit warming to **well below 2 °C**, preferably **1.5 °C**, above pre-industrial levels.

1. **NDCs:** each country sets its own climate action plan; plans must be updated and strengthened.
2. **Transparency and accountability:** regular reporting on emissions and progress.
3. **Climate finance:** developed countries pledged to mobilise USD 100 billion per year for developing countries.
4. **Adaptation and resilience:** preparing for floods, droughts and other climate impacts.
5. **Global stocktake:** a review of progress every 5 years.
"""
NARRATION-1 = Note what is not in the Agreement.
KEY-MESSAGE = There is no single binding emission target for all countries. The Agreement is built on national plans, and it is legally binding on reporting.
NARRATION-2 = Check one number.
ACTIVATION-QUESTION = The global stocktake reviews progress every how many years? (number)
ACTIVATION-ANSWER = 5
ACTIVATION-HINT = It is element five in the list.
ACTIVATION-FEEDBACK = Every 5 years. Each country decides its own contribution and reports on it, and the whole system is reviewed.


[Concept_Explanation_Template_2.md]
SLIDE-TITLE = Net Zero Is Not Zero
NARRATION-0 = You will hear the term net zero in every policy text from now on, so let us be precise. Remember the company in session two one, with its carbon neutral workshop? Jelena compares the two terms.
CONCEPT-A = Net zero
CONCEPT-B = Carbon neutral
CRITERION-1 = Means
A-1 = balancing greenhouse gases emitted with the amount **removed** from the atmosphere
B-1 = a claim that often applies only to CO₂
CRITERION-2 = Covers
A-2 = greenhouse gases
B-2 = often only CO₂
CRITERION-3 = Priority
A-3 = reduce as much as possible, then offset the rest
B-3 = relies more on offsets
CRITERION-4 = Ways of offsetting
A-4 = nature-based solutions, technology (carbon capture and storage), carbon credits (controversial)
B-4 = often credits and tree planting
NARRATION-1 = Here are two examples.
EXAMPLE-A = The EU's legally binding target for 2050
EXAMPLE-B = The company's workshop in Session 2_1: credits and trees, with emissions continuing
NARRATION-2 = And one common error.
MISCONCEPTION = "Net zero means zero emissions." It does not. It is a balance: as much removed as emitted, with the priority on reducing emissions first.
NARRATION-3 = Now you sort them.
STATEMENT-1 = Balances greenhouse gases emitted with those removed
STATEMENT-2 = Often applies only to CO₂
STATEMENT-3 = Reduce as much as possible first, then offset the rest
STATEMENT-4 = Relies more on offsets
CLASSIFY-HINT = Which term covers all greenhouse gases, and which relies more on offsets?
CLASSIFY-FEEDBACK = Net zero is a balance across greenhouse gases, with reduction first. "Carbon neutral" often applies only to CO₂ and relies more on offsets.
