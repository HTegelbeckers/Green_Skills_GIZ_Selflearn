## What Is a Green Economy?

    --{{0}}--
Stefan starts with the best-known term. In 2008, the UN Environment Programme launched the Green Economy Initiative, to motivate policymakers to support environmental investments. This video explains the idea in plain language.

> [!TIP] Watch for

!?[What is a green economy?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VkOtfvhtawA "Greenpeace UK – What is a green economy? (4:08)")

<details>

<summary>Text summary of the video</summary>

In 2008, the UN Environment Programme launched the **Green Economy Initiative** to motivate policymakers to support environmental investments.

**Definition:** "An inclusive green economy is one that improves human well-being and builds social equity while reducing environmental risks and scarcities." (UN Environment 2015)

In 2015, at the UN General Assembly, UN Environment published *Uncovering pathways towards an inclusive green economy*. It describes seven concepts: sharing, circularity, collaboration, solidarity, resilience, opportunity and interdependence.

This approach is seen as an alternative to today's dominant model, which, the argument goes, exacerbates inequalities, encourages waste, triggers resource scarcities and threatens environment and health.

## Criticism of the Green Economy

    --{{0}}--
The green economy is not without critics. Several authors raise the same core objections. It is a strong starting point, but some researchers argue it is not enough.

**What do the critics say? (Unmüßig, Sachs & Fatheuer 2012; Brand 2012)**

    --{{1}}--
First, consumption.

      {{1}}
- **Consumption:** it ignores the need to reduce consumption, assuming we can keep consuming more, just "greener"

    --{{2}}--
Second, growth.

      {{2}}
- **Growth:** it sticks to economic growth as the central goal, offering "more of the same" instead of a fundamental transformation

    --{{3}}--
Third, carbon.

      {{3}}
- **Carbon:** it focuses on decarbonisation instead of all planetary boundaries

    --{{4}}--
Fourth, technology.

      {{4}}
- **Technology:** it relies heavily on technological innovation to solve environmental problems

    --{{5}}--
You do not need to decide today which side you agree with. But you should know that the debate exists.

      {{5}}
> [!TIP] Why this matters
> You do not need to decide today which side you agree with, but you should know that the debate exists. "Stopping growth" is not a criticism of the green economy: it is closer to the degrowth position.

## The Circular Economy

    --{{0}}--
Now to the model behind the workshop. The circular economy is a model of production and consumption that keeps existing materials and products in use for as long as possible. Instead of a line that ends in waste, this image shows a loop.

<div style="display:flex;gap:1.5rem;flex-wrap:wrap;align-items:center;">
<div style="flex:1 1 260px;">

![The circular economy concept: a loop of design, production, use, return and regeneration instead of a linear take-make-dispose chain](img/1280px-The_Circular_Economy_concept.png "The Circular Economy concept – Geissdoerfer et al. – CC BY 4.0 – via Wikimedia Commons")

</div>
<div style="flex:2 1 320px;">

The **circular economy** involves **sharing, leasing, reusing, repairing, refurbishing and recycling** existing materials and products as long as possible. Product life cycles are extended, and waste is reduced to a minimum.

In practice, markets give incentives to **reuse** products rather than scrapping them and extracting new resources. Waste such as clothes, scrap metal and obsolete electronics is returned to the economy or used more efficiently.

</div>
</div>

    --{{1}}--
Why would a ministry care?

      {{1}}
> [!NOTE] Key message
> A circular economy can protect the environment, use resources more wisely, develop new sectors, **create jobs** and new capabilities.

## Green or Circular?

    --{{0}}--
Both approaches are often mentioned together, but they are not the same. Stefan compares them for the green plan.

<!-- data-type="none" -->
| Criterion | Green economy | Circular economy |
|-----------|---------------|---------------|
| Goal | sustainable development without degrading the environment | keep products, materials and resources in use as long as possible |
| Focus and examples | renewable energy, energy efficiency, sustainable agriculture, pollution reduction; solar and wind projects, green buildings, sustainable farming | design, reuse, refurbishing, remanufacturing, recycling; product life extension, industrial symbiosis, recycling systems |
| Model and policy | integrates environmental sustainability into economic policies; regulation, incentives for clean technology, penalties for polluters | from linear "take-make-dispose" to circular "make-use-return"; extended producer responsibility, product-as-a-service, design for longevity |
| Economic benefits | green jobs, sustainable business, green technologies | new business models: leasing, sharing, servitization |

    --{{1}}--
These approaches also create professions.

      {{1}}
- **Green economy:** Solar and wind installers, energy advisors, efficient-building trades, sustainable agriculture
- **Circular economy:** Repair technicians, refurbishment of electronics and machines, recycling and waste management, design for disassembly

    --{{2}}--
Don't confuse them.

      {{2}}
> [!WARNING] Common misconception
> "Green and circular mean the same." Both aim at sustainability, but they work on different parts of the system: clean energy and production on one side, keeping products and materials in use on the other.

## Four More Approaches

    --{{0}}--
Beyond green and circular economies, four more approaches are often discussed. These approaches are not only policy terms. They create and shape professions.

**How familiar are these terms?**

    --{{1}}--
First, corporate social responsibility.

      {{1}}
- **Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR):** a self-regulating business model that helps a company be socially accountable to itself, its stakeholders and the public

    --{{2}}--
Second, doughnut economics.

      {{2}}
- **Doughnut economics:** the economy is a means to meet everyone's needs within planetary boundaries

    --{{3}}--
Third, degrowth.

      {{3}}
- **Degrowth:** reduced material consumption to stay within ecological limits (strong sustainability)

    --{{4}}--
And fourth, net zero, which is a balance, and not zero emissions.

      {{4}}
- **Net zero:** a balance between emissions and removals. It is not the same as zero emissions, and it differs from "carbon neutral"

## Into the Green Plan

    --{{0}}--
Stefan writes the paragraph about the workshop. Serbia has already taken a concrete step in this direction. The Roadmap for Circular Economy was published in 2020, as the first in the Western Balkans. Its aims are to encourage circular business models and job creation.

<div style="display:flex;gap:1.5rem;flex-wrap:wrap;align-items:flex-start;">
<div style="flex:1 1 260px;">

**The Roadmap for Circular Economy in Serbia** is a starting document for dialogue between decision-makers, industry, academia and civil society. Its aims are to encourage circular business models and job creation. The EEA tracks Serbia's **circular material use rate**, and the **Green Agenda for the Western Balkans** includes circular economy as one of its areas.
