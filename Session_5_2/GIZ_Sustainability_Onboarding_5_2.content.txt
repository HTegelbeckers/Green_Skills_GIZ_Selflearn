NARRATION-0 = The green economy is not without critics. Several authors raise the same core objections. It is a strong starting point, but some researchers argue it is not enough.
GUIDING-QUESTION = What do the critics say? (Unmüßig, Sachs & Fatheuer 2012; Brand 2012)
NARRATION-1 = First, consumption.
ASPECT-1 = **Consumption:** it ignores the need to reduce consumption, assuming we can keep consuming more, just "greener"
NARRATION-2 = Second, growth.
ASPECT-2 = **Growth:** it sticks to economic growth as the central goal, offering "more of the same" instead of a fundamental transformation
NARRATION-3 = Third, carbon.
ASPECT-3 = **Carbon:** it focuses on decarbonisation instead of all planetary boundaries
NARRATION-4 = Fourth, technology.
ASPECT-4 = **Technology:** it relies heavily on technological innovation to solve environmental problems
NARRATION-5 = You do not need to decide today which side you agree with. But you should know that the debate exists.
WHY-IT-MATTERS = You do not need to decide today which side you agree with, but you should know that the debate exists. "Stopping growth" is not a criticism of the green economy: it is closer to the degrowth position.

NARRATION-0 = Now to the model behind the workshop. The circular economy is a model of production and consumption that keeps existing materials and products in use for as long as possible. Instead of a line that ends in waste, this image shows a loop.
IMAGE-ALT = The circular economy concept: a loop of design, production, use, return and regeneration instead of a linear take-make-dispose chain
IMAGE-PATH = img/1280px-The_Circular_Economy_concept.png

INTRO-TEXT = """
The **circular economy** involves **sharing, leasing, reusing, repairing, refurbishing and recycling** existing materials and products as long as possible. Product life cycles are extended, and waste is reduced to a minimum.

In practice, markets give incentives to **reuse** products rather than scrapping them and extracting new resources. Waste such as clothes, scrap metal and obsolete electronics is returned to the economy or used more efficiently.
"""

[Concept_Explanation_Template_2.md]
SLIDE-TITLE = Green or Circular?
NARRATION-0 = Both approaches are often mentioned together, but they are not the same. Stefan compares them for the green plan.
CONCEPT-A = Green economy
CONCEPT-B = Circular economy
CRITERION-1 = Goal
A-1 = sustainable development without degrading the environment
B-1 = keep products, materials and resources in use as long as possible
CRITERION-2 = Focus and examples
A-2 = renewable energy, energy efficiency, sustainable agriculture, pollution reduction; solar and wind projects, green buildings, sustainable farming
B-2 = design, reuse, refurbishing, remanufacturing, recycling; product life extension, industrial symbiosis, recycling systems
CRITERION-3 = Model and policy
A-3 = integrates environmental sustainability into economic policies; regulation, incentives for clean technology, penalties for polluters
B-3 = from linear "take-make-dispose" to circular "make-use-return"; extended producer responsibility, product-as-a-service, design for longevity
CRITERION-4 = Economic benefits
A-4 = green jobs, sustainable business, green technologies
B-4 = new business models: leasing, sharing, servitization
NARRATION-1 = These approaches also create professions.
EXAMPLE-A = Solar and wind installers, energy advisors, efficient-building trades, sustainable agriculture
EXAMPLE-B = Repair technicians, refurbishment of electronics and machines, recycling and waste management, design for disassembly
NARRATION-2 = Don't confuse them.
MISCONCEPTION = "Green and circular mean the same." Both aim at sustainability, but they work on different parts of the system: clean energy and production on one side, keeping products and materials in use on the other.
NARRATION-3 = Now you sort them.
STATEMENT-1 = Solar and wind installer
STATEMENT-2 = Repair technician for electronics and machines
STATEMENT-3 = Incentives for clean technology and penalties for polluters
STATEMENT-4 = New business models such as leasing and sharing
CLASSIFY-HINT = Does it serve clean energy and production, or does it keep products in use?
CLASSIFY-FEEDBACK = The green economy focuses on clean energy and production and steers it with regulation and incentives. The circular economy keeps products and materials in use and builds new business models.


[Content_Introduction_Template_2.md]
SLIDE-TITLE = Four More Approaches
NARRATION-0 = Beyond green and circular economies, four more approaches are often discussed. These approaches are not only policy terms. They create and shape professions.
GUIDING-QUESTION = How familiar are these terms?
NARRATION-1 = First, corporate social responsibility.
ASPECT-1 = **Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR):** a self-regulating business model that helps a company be socially accountable to itself, its stakeholders and the public
NARRATION-2 = Second, doughnut economics.
ASPECT-2 = **Doughnut economics:** the economy is a means to meet everyone's needs within planetary boundaries
NARRATION-3 = Third, degrowth.
ASPECT-3 = **Degrowth:** reduced material consumption to stay within ecological limits (strong sustainability)
NARRATION-4 = And fourth, net zero, which is a balance, and not zero emissions.
ASPECT-4 = **Net zero:** a balance between emissions and removals. It is not the same as zero emissions, and it differs from "carbon neutral"
NARRATION-5 = Take a moment to look at your own surroundings.
WHY-IT-MATTERS = Which approach is most visible in the companies and sectors you work with today: circular economy, green economy, CSR, or none of them so far?
