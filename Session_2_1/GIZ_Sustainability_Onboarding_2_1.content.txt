NARRATION-0 = The word is used in many ways. Milica collects five definitions, from everyday language to education policy. Most combine environment, time and people, but they stress different parts.
IMAGE-ALT = Classic diagram of sustainable development showing environment, society and economy and their overlaps

<!-- data-type="none" -->
| Definition | What it stresses |
|---|---|
| **Oxford dictionary** | Using natural products and energy without harming the environment; the ability to continue for a long time |
| **Brundtland / WCED (1987)** | "… development that meets the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs." |
| **"Enough, for everyone, forever."** | Short African paraphrase of the Brundtland idea, used in GIZ trainings: everyone is at the centre |
| **Carlowitz (1713)** | "continuous, steady and sustained use" of the forest: cut only what grows back |

"""

NARRATION-2 = Let us check the definition you will hear most often.

ACTIVATION-ANSWER = generations
ACTIVATION-HINT = It is about the people who come after us.
ACTIVATION-FEEDBACK = "… future generations." Brundtland is the only definition here that explicitly names them.

SLIDE-TITLE = Whose "Should"?
NARRATION-0 = Now the key idea of this session. Milica asks Jelena: who decides which definition is correct? Jelena reminds her of a sentence by Juliana Hilf.
IMAGE-ALT = Classic diagram of sustainable development showing environment, society and economy and their overlaps
IMAGE-PATH = img/1280px-Sustainable_development.svg.png
IMAGE-CAPTION = Sustainable development – Johann Dréo (nojhan) – CC BY-SA 3.0
SCENARIO-TEXT = """
**Normative** means that the concept says how society **should** develop. It is a social and political guideline, not a technical measurement. Because it is about what *should* be, it is **contested**: there are scientific and political controversies about what sustainability means in practice.
"""
NARRATION-1 = Here is the sentence.
QUOTE = The concept of sustainable development is not the result of scientific research; instead, it is an ethically grounded concept.
QUOTE-SOURCE = Juliana Hilf (2024), Introduction to the Sustainability Approach
NARRATION-2 = That leaves Milica with a practical problem.
PROBLEM = If no measurement can settle what sustainability means, how can a school decide whether the company's label is acceptable?
POE-TITLE = What Follows from "Normative"?
NARRATION-POE-0 = Let us test the idea.
PREDICT-QUESTION = What does it mean that sustainability is a *normative* concept?
PREDICT-CORRECT = It says how society should develop, so it is open to debate and negotiation.
PREDICT-WRONG-1 = It can be measured with standard scientific indicators.
PREDICT-WRONG-2 = It is a legal rule that all EU member states must follow.
PREDICT-HINT = Think about the word "should" – and who decides what "good development" means.
NARRATION-POE-1 = This has two consequences for people who work with the concept: reflectivity, and negotiation and societal learning.
OBSERVATION = Two consequences follow. **Reflectivity:** you have to think about your own values and the values behind a policy or school decision. **Negotiation and societal learning:** the transition relies on constant negotiation between different goals and on learning together (UNESCO 2022). That is why the term "(great) transformation" became central: sustainability is not one reform, but a long process of societal change (WBGU 2011).
NARRATION-POE-2 = Now bring it home. Think of a real example from your own work.
EXPLAIN-PROMPT = Where in your work do you have to negotiate between goals, for example jobs today versus climate goals? A sentence or two is enough.


[Concept_Explanation_Template_2.md]
SLIDE-TITLE = Justice: Between and Within
NARRATION-0 = Where does the should come from? At the core of the normative idea are two forms of justice. Inter means between. Intra means within.
CONCEPT-A = Intergenerational justice
CONCEPT-B = Intragenerational justice
CRITERION-1 = Word
A-1 = *inter* = between generations
B-1 = *intra* = within the generations alive now
CRITERION-2 = Responsibility for
A-2 = future generations
B-2 = different people living today
CRITERION-3 = Claim
A-3 = they must also be able to satisfy their needs
B-3 = all humans should enjoy a decent life that at least satisfies fundamental needs
CRITERION-4 = Looks across
A-4 = time
B-4 = people
NARRATION-1 = Here is what this could mean for a school like Milica's.
EXAMPLE-A = Keeping the workshop running for students who are not yet born
EXAMPLE-B = Green skills also reaching schools in less well-resourced regions
NARRATION-2 = A common mistake is to think that the second form is only about poor countries.
MISCONCEPTION = "Intragenerational justice is only about countries with low incomes." It applies to everyone living today. And the two forms are linked: decisions about resources and education affect people today *and* tomorrow.
NARRATION-3 = Now you decide. Which form of justice does each statement describe?
STATEMENT-1 = Future generations must also be able to satisfy their needs.
STATEMENT-2 = All humans should be able to enjoy a decent life today.
STATEMENT-3 = The programmes we design now should not use up what later cohorts need.
STATEMENT-4 = A school in a poor region should get the same chance of green skills as one in a rich region.
CLASSIFY-HINT = Inter = between generations. Intra = within the people living today.
CLASSIFY-FEEDBACK = Statements 1 and 3 look across time. Statements 2 and 4 look across people living today.

[Concept_Explanation_Template_2.md]
SLIDE-TITLE = Weak or Strong?
NARRATION-0 = Milica puts the two readings side by side, with examples from real projects.
CONCEPT-A = Weak sustainability
CONCEPT-B = Strong sustainability
CRITERION-1 = Natural capital
A-1 = can be **substituted** by human-made capital
B-1 = is **non-substitutable** and must be preserved
CRITERION-2 = Focus
A-2 = **efficiency**: do more with less
B-2 = **sufficiency**: use less, protect what cannot be replaced
CRITERION-3 = Examples
A-3 = carbon offsetting by airlines; GDP growth via green technology; biodiversity compensation schemes
B-3 = ecosystem protection (no-use zones); agroecology and local food systems; degrowth strategies
CRITERION-4 = Test question
A-4 = Does it *replace* nature with technology or credits?
B-4 = Does it *preserve*, or *reduce* use?
NARRATION-1 = Here is one example of each, explained.
EXAMPLE-A = Carbon offsetting: emissions continue, relying on tree planting or credits instead of reduction.
EXAMPLE-B = No-use zones: critical ecosystems such as old-growth forests are preserved, not traded off.
NARRATION-2 = And a warning about a shortcut.
MISCONCEPTION = "Offsetting means emissions are gone." Under offsetting, emissions continue: tree planting or credits stand in for reduction. Habitat destroyed in one place is only "offset" by protection elsewhere.
NARRATION-3 = Now you sort them. Replace, or preserve and reduce?
STATEMENT-1 = Carbon offsetting by airlines
STATEMENT-2 = Agroecology and local food systems
STATEMENT-3 = Biodiversity compensation schemes
STATEMENT-4 = Degrowth strategies
CLASSIFY-HINT = Does the example replace nature with technology or credits (weak), or does it preserve or reduce (strong)?
CLASSIFY-FEEDBACK = The weak examples rely on substitution: emissions continue, or habitat is compensated elsewhere. The strong examples refuse to trade off: farming works with natural systems, and degrowth reduces material consumption to stay within ecological limits.

GUIDING-QUESTION = What does sustainability look like from a household or a village?

ASPECT-1 = **Local conditions of life:** the starting point is everyday life, not the global economy
NARRATION-2 = It asks about livelihood security.
ASPECT-2 = **Livelihood:** "the capabilities, assets (including both material and social resources) and activities required for a means of living"
NARRATION-3 = It looks at the everyday experience of women.
ASPECT-3 = **Everyday experiences of women:** this view was shaped by women's movements in developing countries
NARRATION-4 = And it takes the subsistence economy seriously.
ASPECT-4 = **Subsistence economy:** producing for your own household first is of major importance in many of these regions
NARRATION-5 = Why does this matter? Because a definition that starts from global growth can miss what a farming family actually needs.
WHY-IT-MATTERS = A definition that starts from global growth and capital can miss what a farming family, a small workshop or a rural community actually needs to live. The livelihoods view keeps those realities in the picture.
