[Content_Introduction_Template_1.md]
SLIDE-TITLE = The Roots of the Word
NARRATION-0 = The modern German word Nachhaltigkeit goes back to the noun Nachhalt. It describes an old survival strategy. Do not use everything today. Keep something for a hard winter.
IMAGE-ALT = Title page of Sylvicultura oeconomica, 1713
IMAGE-PATH = img/Carlowitz_Sylvicultura.jpg
IMAGE-CAPTION = Sylvicultura oeconomica, title page – Hans Carl von Carlowitz (1713) – public domain – via Wikimedia Commons
INTRO-TEXT = """
The modern German word *Nachhaltigkeit* goes back to the noun *Nachhalt*. It originally meant "support, backing" or "what is held back": reserves kept for times of scarcity. It describes an old survival strategy of **precaution and self-limitation**.

The adjective *nachhaltig* / *nachhalten* is documented in German by the end of the 18th century. [CHECK: Juliana – confirm the dating; Carlowitz used "nachaltende" already in 1713]
"""
NARRATION-1 = This explains a lot.
KEY-MESSAGE = At its root, sustainability is about **not using everything at once**.
NARRATION-2 = Check the meaning.
ACTIVATION-QUESTION = *Nachhalt* originally meant what is held back for times of scarcity. What is the English word for this? (one word, plural)
ACTIVATION-ANSWER = reserves
ACTIVATION-HINT = Think of what you keep aside for a hard winter.
ACTIVATION-FEEDBACK = Reserves. It is a strategy of precaution and self-limitation, not a tree or a trade rule.


[Content_Introduction_Template_1.md]
SLIDE-TITLE = Carlowitz and the Wood Crisis
NARRATION-0 = Now to the man himself. In the eighteenth century, German-speaking territories faced a severe wood shortage. In 1713, Carlowitz published the first systematic description of sustainable forestry.
IMAGE-ALT = Portrait of Hans Carl von Carlowitz, engraving
IMAGE-PATH = img/Hans_Carl_von_Carlowitz.jpg
IMAGE-CAPTION = Hans Carl von Carlowitz – Martin Bernigeroth (engraving, 18th century) – public domain
INTRO-TEXT = """
In the 18th century, German-speaking territories faced a severe **wood shortage**. Forests were over-used for timber trade, war, mining and smelting, tanning and glass-making. Forests shrank, and erosion and soil degradation threatened whole regions.

**Hans Carl von Carlowitz (1645–1714)** was the Saxon chief mining official (*Oberberghauptmann*) in Freiberg. In **1713** he published *Sylvicultura oeconomica*. He called for a "continuous, steady and *nachaltende* (sustained) use" of forests.

He also argued **economically**: a country that destroys its forests destroys the basis of its own economy. For him, sustainability was a smart long-term strategy, not altruism.
"""
NARRATION-1 = His core rule was simple.
KEY-MESSAGE = **Cut only as much wood as can grow back** through reforestation.
NARRATION-2 = Check the year.
ACTIVATION-QUESTION = In which year did Carlowitz publish *Sylvicultura oeconomica*? (4 digits)
ACTIVATION-ANSWER = 1713
ACTIVATION-HINT = It was the year before he died (he lived 1645–1714).
ACTIVATION-FEEDBACK = 1713. It was a response to the wood shortage of his time.

[Content_Introduction_Template_2.md]
SLIDE-TITLE = Four Core Components
NARRATION-0 = Carlowitz' idea was developed further by Georg Ludwig Hartig, in his work of 1804. Hartig wrote that the present generation should use the forest as much as possible, but so that the descendants can draw at least as much advantage from it. It contains four core components.
GUIDING-QUESTION = What does forest sustainability consist of?
NARRATION-1 = First, long-term thinking.
ASPECT-1 = **Long-term thinking:** the forest's services (timber, water storage, erosion protection, habitat) must be secured continuously; planning over decades to centuries
NARRATION-2 = Second, social obligation.
ASPECT-2 = **Social obligation:** forests also serve public functions; public interests can limit private property rights
NARRATION-3 = Third, economy.
ASPECT-3 = **Economy:** sustainability does not mean "no use", but economic use with planned protection of the resource
NARRATION-4 = And fourth, responsibility.
ASPECT-4 = **Responsibility:** awareness of the forest's importance for future generations
NARRATION-5 = And that is an early form of a very modern idea.
WHY-IT-MATTERS = **Hartig (1764–1837)** wrote in his work of 1804 that the present generation should use the forest, but so "that the descendants can draw at least as much advantage from it". This is an early form of **intergenerational justice**: fairness towards future generations.
NARRATION-6 = Which of these components did you already know?


[Concept_Explanation_Template_2.md]
SLIDE-TITLE = Live from the Interest
NARRATION-0 = The forest idea is often explained with a money metaphor. A forest is managed sustainably if the annual harvest equals the annual growth. This is also how Milica looks at her budget.
CONCEPT-A = Interest
CONCEPT-B = Capital
CRITERION-1 = In a forest
A-1 = the annual growth
B-1 = the stock of trees
CRITERION-2 = Sustainable use
A-2 = may be used
B-2 = stays intact
CRITERION-3 = In a school workshop
A-3 = the value the workshop produces year after year
B-3 = the working capacity: machines, trained staff, the budget line for maintenance
CRITERION-4 = Warning sign
A-4 = the harvest of a year is larger than the growth
B-4 = the stock is shrinking
NARRATION-1 = Here are two examples.
EXAMPLE-A = The wood that regrows in one year
EXAMPLE-B = The pipeline of qualified teachers, which must not shrink below what schools need
NARRATION-2 = There is one error to avoid.
MISCONCEPTION = "Sustainability means no use." Carlowitz' rule was to use the resource, but not to destroy the stock that produces it. Profit maximisation, on the other hand, is the opposite of the idea.
NARRATION-3 = Now you sort them.
STATEMENT-1 = What may be used each year
STATEMENT-2 = What must be kept intact
STATEMENT-3 = The annual growth of a forest
STATEMENT-4 = The stock of experienced teachers
CLASSIFY-HINT = "Live from the interest, not from the capital." Which one do you use, and which one do you keep?
CLASSIFY-FEEDBACK = The growth is the interest, and it can be used. The stock is the capital, and it must stay intact. For a long time this principle stayed in specialised fields such as forestry. Only in the second half of the 20th century did it become a global idea.
