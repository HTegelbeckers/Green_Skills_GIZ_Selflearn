## The Evidence in Seven Points

    --{{0}}--
The scientific community summarises its findings in regular assessment reports. The IPCC Synthesis Report of 2023 lists seven key points. Keep an eye on the last one. The warming stripes show the shift: each band is one year from 1850 to 2018.

<div style="display:flex;gap:1.5rem;flex-wrap:wrap;align-items:center;">
<div style="flex:1 1 260px;">

![Warming stripes: one colour band per year from 1850 to 2018, shifting from blue to red](img/80px-20181204_Warming_stripes__global__WMO__1850-2018__-_Climate_Lab_Book__Ed_Hawkins_.png "Warming stripes – Ed Hawkins – CC BY-SA 4.0. Each band is one year, from 1850 to 2018.")

</div>
<div style="flex:2 1 320px;">

**IPCC Synthesis Report (2023), seven key points:**

1. Warming is about 1.1 °C.
2. Extreme weather hits people where they are most vulnerable, in the Global South.
3. Climate change is becoming the most important cause of species extinction.
4. 1.5 °C is the limit that decides about life, and the world is about to fail here.
5. Emissions must fall faster.
6. Climate protection costs, but the climate crisis costs more.
7. **A liveable future is still possible.**

</div>

## Why Wording Matters

    --{{0}}--
Now to the heart of this session. The same facts can be told with different words. Jelena puts both options side by side.

<!-- data-type="none" -->
| Criterion | "Climate change" | "Climate crisis" |
|-----------|---------------|---------------|
| Sounds | neutral and slow | urgent: we need to act now |
| Related terms | "global warming" | "climate emergency", "climate breakdown", "global heating" |
| Job in a sentence | the neutral, scientific description of long-term shifts | frames the situation as demanding immediate action |
| Risk | may sound too slow or too safe | can create fear or fatigue, and people may stop listening |

    --{{1}}--
Here is how each term could be used in a document.

      {{1}}
- **"Climate change":** "Climate change means constant and extreme changes of the climate."
- **"Climate crisis":** "This is why we treat the climate crisis as a priority for VET."

    --{{2}}--
Here is the balance this training uses.

      {{2}}
> [!WARNING] Common misconception
> "You have to pick one, and the other is wrong." Both are correct: they do different jobs. The balance used here: **name the problem honestly, and show that a liveable future is still possible** (IPCC key point 7).

## For the People in Obrenovac

    --{{0}}--
Jelena thinks of May 2014. So the honest question is not climate change or climate crisis. For the people in Obrenovac in 2014, was this climate change or a crisis? Note the careful wording: a single flood cannot be attributed one-to-one to climate change.

<div style="display:flex;gap:1.5rem;flex-wrap:wrap;align-items:flex-start;">
<div style="flex:1 1 260px;">

![Aerial view of flooded fields beside a wide river near Obrenovac, Serbia, 2014](img/1280px-Flood_in_Obrenovac__2014.jpg "Flood in Obrenovac, 2014 – Gmihail at Serbian Wikipedia – CC BY-SA 3.0 RS – via Wikimedia Commons")

</div>
<div style="flex:2 1 320px;">

In May 2014, floods hit Serbia. Obrenovac, near Belgrade, was one of the worst-affected towns. About 32,000 families were forced from their homes, and energy and agriculture were among the hardest-hit sectors. A single flood cannot be attributed one-to-one to climate change. But for the people in Obrenovac, this was clearly a **crisis**: homes, jobs and livelihoods were at stake. That is what the word is for.

<!-- data-type="none" -->
| Figure | Value |
|--------|-------|
| People affected | more than 1.6 million, about 22 % of the population |
| Lives lost | 57 (World Bank figure; other sources give 51–60) |
| Damage and jobs | more than EUR 1 billion (about 4–5 % of GDP); about 51,800 jobs temporarily lost (ILO) |

## Your Advice to Jelena

    --{{0}}--
First, make Jelena's decision. A good answer gives a reason.

**1 · Answer:** Which word do you choose for the official introduction – and how do you handle the colleague who wants the other one?

[[___ ___]]

<details>

<summary>✅ Model answer</summary>

There is no single right answer. In a ministry document, "climate change" keeps the text factual, and "climate crisis" signals a priority. A strong introduction can use both: name the change first, then explain why it is a crisis. A school developer might add that the pair of words also decides how strongly you push a topic in teacher training. A principal might prefer "crisis" in an internal letter to mobilise staff. The wording sets the tone of the whole text.

</details>
