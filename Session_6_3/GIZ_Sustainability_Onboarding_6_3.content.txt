NARRATION-0 = Climate change, planetary boundaries and the SDGs are global problems. But the actions that solve them happen locally: in a school building, a workshop, a municipality, a company. The two levels need each other.
IMAGE-ALT = Solar installation technician working on a rooftop

INTRO-TEXT = """
Global challenges need action where people live and work. The two levels need each other:

- **Global frameworks give direction.** The SDGs and the Paris Agreement show *where* the world is heading. The SDGs are a good framework to link any greening strategy, project or syllabus with global goals.
- **Local action makes it real.** A green campus, a greened curriculum, a partnership with a local company: this is where change becomes visible and measurable.
"""

ACTIVATION-ANSWER = local
ACTIVATION-HINT = It is in the name: think global, act …
ACTIVATION-FEEDBACK = Local: in a school, a company, a municipality. The SDGs then show why the local action matters globally.

SLIDE-TITLE = The Whole-Institution Approach
NARRATION-0 = Milica knows that a solar system on the roof is not enough. Greening a VET institution is more than changing the curriculum. UNESCO-UNEVOC describes a whole-institution approach. Besides the curriculum, it includes four more things.
GUIDING-QUESTION = What belongs to greening a whole institution, besides the curriculum?
NARRATION-1 = First, how it is run.
ASPECT-1 = **Integrated management and governance**
NARRATION-2 = Second, what it stands for.
ASPECT-2 = **A sustainability ethos:** the shared values of the institution
NARRATION-3 = Third, who it works with.
ASPECT-3 = **Community and stakeholder engagement**
NARRATION-4 = Fourth, how far it looks ahead, and how it checks progress.
ASPECT-4 = **Long-term planning, with sustainability monitoring and evaluation**
NARRATION-5 = And who decides? Everyone, together.
WHY-IT-MATTERS = All stakeholders – **leadership, teachers, learners, administration** – jointly develop a vision and a plan. Networks of institutions enable peer-to-peer learning. Greening works *within* existing programmes: it does not demand replacing the entire curriculum.

SLIDE-TITLE = Four Steps of Greening
NARRATION-0 = UNEVOC describes four steps. Milica applies them to her school. They form a cycle, not a one-off project.
STEP-1 = Understanding
STEP-2 = Planning
STEP-3 = Implementing
STEP-4 = Monitoring
NARRATION-1 = First, everyone understands the process.
STEP-TEXT-1 = Leadership, teachers, learners and administration understand the greening process. Only then can they jointly develop the plan.
NARRATION-2 = Second, there is a plan.
STEP-TEXT-2 = An institutional greening plan with short-, medium- and long-term goals.
NARRATION-3 = Third, measures are put into practice.
STEP-TEXT-3 = In five action fields: **green campus** (energy, water, waste, buildings), **green curriculum**, **green community** (partnerships with companies and the municipality), **green culture** and **green research**.
NARRATION-4 = Fourth, progress is checked, and the plan is adjusted. After that, the cycle usually starts again.
STEP-TEXT-4 = Monitoring and assessment: check progress and adjust the plan. After that, the cycle usually starts again. That is what makes it a long-term approach.

SLIDE-TITLE = From Idea to Action: Five Steps
NARRATION-0 = How do you turn an idea like Milica's roof into a real project? The micro-project method gives you five steps. Skipping an early step is the most common reason small green projects stall.
IMAGE-ALT = Solar installation technician working on a rooftop
IMAGE-PATH = img/1280px-Solar_installation_technician_on_rooftop__5392894792_.jpg
IMAGE-CAPTION = Solar installation technician on rooftop – CoCreatr (Flickr) – CC BY-SA 2.0
INTRO-TEXT = """
1. **Profile:** name of your institution or department; field of work; core tasks.
2. **Assessment of the institution:** environmental and social practices; strengths, weaknesses, opportunities. Three **lenses** help: resource efficiency; climate relevance; occupational safety and health.
3. **Defining project outcomes:** aligned with the SDGs and sustainability.
4. **Assessing resources:** which are relevant, which do you have, which do you need?
5. **Project plan:** a small-scale green initiative based on the outcomes.
"""
NARRATION-1 = Here is the pattern to notice.
KEY-MESSAGE = Steps 1 and 2 look at yourself, step 3 at the goals, step 4 at what you have, and step 5 is doing.
NARRATION-2 = Check step three.
ACTIVATION-QUESTION = Step 3 of the micro-project is defining project … that are aligned with the SDGs. (one word)
ACTIVATION-ANSWER = outcomes
ACTIVATION-HINT = It is the third item in the list.
ACTIVATION-FEEDBACK = Outcomes: the results the project should deliver. Step 5 is then built from these outcomes, not the other way round.

NARRATION-Q-0 = Step three: your outcomes.
QUESTION-1 = **Step 3, Outcomes:** what outcomes should your initiative deliver, and which SDGs do they align with?
MODEL-ANSWER-1 = """
Formulate outcomes as results, not activities: not "install solar panels", but "students can plan and maintain a PV system" or "the school measures its energy use and reduces it". Then name the SDGs. For energy and climate topics, these are usually the goals about clean energy and climate action. If you cannot name an SDG, the outcome is probably too vague.
"""
NARRATION-Q-1 = Step four: your resources.
QUESTION-2 = **Step 4, Resources:** which resources are relevant for your outcomes? Which does your institution already have, and which does it need?
MODEL-ANSWER-2 = """
Resources are more than money: people (a teacher, a caretaker, a company partner), knowledge, equipment, space and time. A useful pattern: "We **have** X and Y, we **need** Z, and Z can come from a local company partner or from a network with another school." Naming the gap now prevents the project from stalling in step 5.
"""
NARRATION-Q-2 = Step five: your plan. This is the most important one.
QUESTION-3 = **Step 5, Project plan:** write your small-scale green initiative in 5–8 sentences: what, who, when, and how you will check it.
MODEL-ANSWER-3 = """
Three worked examples (constructed):

- **Ministry officer:** "Within the revision of occupational standards for electricians, add one learning outcome on PV systems and energy efficiency (SDG 7, 13); test with two pilot schools; monitor with the school developers."
- **School developer:** "Set up a network of five VET schools that review one curriculum each with the three lenses; share results in a peer meeting each term."
- **Principal:** "Name a sustainability team (teacher, student, caretaker, company partner); measure electricity and water use for three months; choose one green-campus measure and one curriculum project."

All three have a concrete first step, named actors, and a way to check progress.
