# GIZ Sustainability Onboarding 5 – Economic Issues

**Estimated time:** 10–15 minutes · **Format:** main topic – short overview and entry point for its subtopics

After this session you can:

- examine economic perspectives on sustainability, including growth, limits to growth, and alternative approaches to green economies,
- distinguish the two levels at which sustainability is discussed in economics – the whole economic system and individual companies,
- orient yourself in the main economic approaches (green economy, circular economy, doughnut economics, degrowth) and know which subtopic to start with.

## Why this topic matters

Sustainability is often discussed as an environmental question. But it is also an economic one. The economy decides how resources are used, how much is produced, and who benefits. Two levels of analysis and action matter:

- **Sustainability economics** looks at the economic system as a whole – growth, resources, ecosystem functions.
- **Sustainability management** looks at individual companies – for example Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR).

Both levels need guidance: how to use regenerative and non-regenerative resources, how to keep ecosystem functions (absorbing waste and emissions, securing general ecosystem functions, human well-being) intact.

The question is not only *how much* the economy grows, but *what the economy is for*.

Before you watch: Think about what an economy should do for people and for the planet. The video raises exactly that question. Watch for how the speaker defines the purpose of the economy, and how that purpose connects to environmental limits.

**Check:** Which conflict between SDGs is often named in the sustainability debate?

[[?]] Think about what each approach does with materials and consumption.

- [ [Green economy] [Circular economy] [Doughnut economics] [Degrowth] ]
- [ ( )                (X)                 ( )                ( )             ]  Keep materials in use – reuse, repair, refurbish, recycle

## Learning journal

> **Learning journal:** Which economic approach do you think fits the Serbian economy best today – and which one in 2050? Note one reason for each choice.

<details>
<summary>Compare with an expert view</summary>

There is no single correct answer – but there are good reasons for each approach. A ministry officer might favour the **green economy** today, because it improves well-being and social equity while reducing environmental risks – a goal that fits current policy work. A school developer might prefer the **circular economy**, because repair and refurbishment skills are directly transferable to VET programmes and local businesses. A principal might see **degrowth** as a long-term constraint to plan around, even if it is not the dominant policy today. The key question is not which approach is "right", but which one fits the context – today's institutions, local industry, and the time horizon you are planning for.

</details>
