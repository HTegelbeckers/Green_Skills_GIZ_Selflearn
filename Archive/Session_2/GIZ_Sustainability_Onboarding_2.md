# GIZ Sustainability Onboarding 2 – Basic Concepts and Policies

**Estimated time:** 10–15 minutes · **Format:** main topic overview – short video, one image, a self-assessment, a warm-up check and a journal prompt

After this session you can:

- define sustainability as a **normative concept** and say why it is contested,
- distinguish the key **models of sustainability** (three pillars, doughnut, SDG "wedding cake"),
- relate these concepts and models to **international policy frameworks** (SDGs, Paris Agreement, European Green Deal) and Serbia's climate law.

## Why this topic matters

Sustainability is not only a word. It is a **normative concept**: a social and political guideline about how society *should* develop. That makes it contested – there are scientific and political controversies about it, and there is no single scientifically "correct" definition. The Brundtland definition from 1987 is the common reference point, but how we apply the idea depends on the models we use and the policies we follow.

This matters for you because these frameworks decide what happens in the VET sector: which skills are taught, which schools receive support, and which occupations your schools train for. The Western Balkan leaders endorsed the **Green Agenda for the Western Balkans** in the Sofia Declaration (10 Nov 2020), and Serbia adopted its **Law on Climate Change** on 18 March 2021. All of this builds on the concepts and policies you will meet in this topic.

## Learning journal

> **Learning journal:** Which of the three frameworks – the SDGs, the Paris Agreement or the European Green Deal – matters most for a VET ministry in Serbia, and why?

<details>
<summary>Compare with an expert view</summary>

There is no wrong answer – but here are three valid readings. A **ministry officer** may point to the **Paris Agreement**: it is legally binding on reporting, so Serbia has concrete obligations (NDCs, reviews) that the education system must support. A **school developer** may choose the **SDGs**: they are voluntary and broad, which makes them a useful common language to connect green skills with wider development goals in curriculum work. A **principal** may pick the **European Green Deal**: its EU-wide reach and tools (EU laws and Green Deal policies) are the part that directly shapes funding and standards for schools. In practice, all three matter – the difference is which one you need to act on first.

</details>
