## Model 1: The Three Pillars

    --{{0}}--
Milica starts with the best-known model. It describes sustainability as three dimensions that must be balanced. It is easy to communicate, but look closely at what the picture suggests.

<div style="display:flex;gap:1.5rem;flex-wrap:wrap;align-items:center;">
<div style="flex:1 1 260px;">

![Three overlapping circles representing the ecological, social and economic dimensions of sustainability](img/1280px-Sustainable_development.svg.png "Sustainable development – Johann Dréo (nojhan) – CC BY-SA 3.0 – via Wikimedia Commons")

</div>
<div style="flex:2 1 320px;">

Three dimensions must be balanced: **ecological**, **social** and **economic**. Purvis, Mao and Robinson (2019) searched for the conceptual origins of this model.

**Strengths:** it is easy to communicate, and it shows that sustainability is more than environmental protection.

**Critique:** the picture suggests that the dimensions are equal and can be **traded against each other**, for example economic gains "compensating" for ecological damage. Some authors therefore add a **fourth dimension**, such as culture or governance (Michelsen et al. 2016; Mulligan 2018).

</div>
</div>

Here is the key point.

      {{1}}
> [!NOTE] Key message
> The pillar picture invites "balancing". That is its strength in conversation and its weakness in decisions.

## Model 2: The Nested Model

    --{{0}}--
The nested model, sometimes called the Russian-doll model, draws the same three dimensions differently. Nothing can happen outside the environment.

<div style="display:flex;gap:1.5rem;flex-wrap:wrap;align-items:center;">
<div style="flex:1 1 260px;">

![Nested circles showing the economy inside society and society inside the environment](img/1280px-Nested_sustainability-v2.svg.png "Nested sustainability-v2 – KTucker – CC BY-SA 3.0 – via Wikimedia Commons")

</div>
<div style="flex:2 1 320px;">

The **economy sits inside society**, and **society sits inside the environment**. The economy depends on society, and both depend on the environment.

This is the counter-image to equal pillars: the environment sets the frame. It links to the idea of **strong sustainability** from Session 2_1: the ecological dimension is not exchangeable.

</div>
</div>

    --{{1}}--
The picture is about dependence.

      {{1}}
> [!NOTE] Key message
> The nested model is a picture of dependence, so trade-offs are limited: the environment does not sit next to the others as an equal partner.

## Model 3: The Doughnut

    --{{0}}--
Kate Raworth's doughnut draws two boundaries. Look at the numbered parts one by one.

<div style="position:relative;display:inline-block;width:600px;max-width:100%;">

![The doughnut model: a green ring between an inner social foundation and an outer ecological ceiling](img/1280px-Doughnut__economic_model_.jpg)

<span style="position:absolute;left:48%;top:68.3%;transform:translate(-50%,-50%);background:#c92a2a;color:#fff;border-radius:50%;width:1.8em;height:1.8em;line-height:1.8em;text-align:center;font-weight:bold;">1</span>
<span style="position:absolute;left:24%;top:49.4%;transform:translate(-50%,-50%);background:#c92a2a;color:#fff;border-radius:50%;width:1.8em;height:1.8em;line-height:1.8em;text-align:center;font-weight:bold;">2</span>
<span style="position:absolute;left:48%;top:78.3%;transform:translate(-50%,-50%);background:#c92a2a;color:#fff;border-radius:50%;width:1.8em;height:1.8em;line-height:1.8em;text-align:center;font-weight:bold;">3</span>

</div>

    --{{1}}--
Number one is the inner ring, the social foundation.

      {{1}}
**① Social foundation:** The inner ring: the minimum every person needs, for example food, health, education and work. Falling short of it is a **shortfall**.

    --{{2}}--
Number two is the safe and just space, between the rings.

      {{2}}
**② Safe and just space:** The ring **between** the two boundaries. The economy is a means of staying here, not a pillar of equal weight.

    --{{3}}--
Number three is the outer ring, the ecological ceiling.

      {{3}}
**③ Ecological ceiling:** The outer ring: the planetary limits humanity should not overshoot. Going beyond it is an **overshoot**. Both shortfall and overshoot are failures, so the doughnut does **not** allow trade-offs. The Doughnut Economics Action Lab describes 12 dimensions of the social foundation and 9 planetary boundaries of the ceiling. [CHECK: Juliana – confirm we want these numbers on the page]

## Pillars or Doughnut?

    --{{0}}--
Milica compares the two models that matter most for her board. The nested model sits in between: its trade-offs are limited, because the environment sets the frame.

<!-- data-type="none" -->
| Criterion | Three pillars | Doughnut |
|-----------|---------------|---------------|
| Picture | three equal pillars or overlapping circles | a ring between a floor and a ceiling |
| Trade-offs | possible ("balance") | not allowed: two hard limits |
| Fits | weak sustainability | strong sustainability and social justice |
| Good for | starting a conversation | decisions involving limits |

    --{{1}}--
Here is how each model frames the coal region.

      {{1}}
- **Three pillars:** Jobs in coal regions (social, economic) can be "balanced" against emissions (ecological).
- **Doughnut:** Decent jobs for coal workers and the climate are both hard limits. The question becomes *how* to move into the safe space: a just transition, new green skills.

    --{{2}}--
Here is a common error.

      {{2}}
> [!WARNING] Common misconception
> "The nested model shows that the economy is the most important part." It shows the opposite: the economy depends on society and on the environment.
