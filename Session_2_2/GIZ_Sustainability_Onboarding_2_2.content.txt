SLIDE-TITLE = Model 1: The Three Pillars
NARRATION-0 = Milica starts with the best-known model. It describes sustainability as three dimensions that must be balanced. It is easy to communicate, but look closely at what the picture suggests.
IMAGE-ALT = Three overlapping circles representing the ecological, social and economic dimensions of sustainability
IMAGE-PATH = img/1280px-Sustainable_development.svg.png
IMAGE-CAPTION = Sustainable development – Johann Dréo (nojhan) – CC BY-SA 3.0 – via Wikimedia Commons
INTRO-TEXT = """
Three dimensions must be balanced: **ecological**, **social** and **economic**. Purvis, Mao and Robinson (2019) searched for the conceptual origins of this model.

**Strengths:** it is easy to communicate, and it shows that sustainability is more than environmental protection.

**Critique:** the picture suggests that the dimensions are equal and can be **traded against each other**, for example economic gains "compensating" for ecological damage. Some authors therefore add a **fourth dimension**, such as culture or governance (Michelsen et al. 2016; Mulligan 2018).
"""
NARRATION-1 = Here is the key point.
KEY-MESSAGE = The pillar picture invites "balancing". That is its strength in conversation and its weakness in decisions.
NARRATION-2 = Which dimension is missing in this sentence?
ACTIVATION-QUESTION = The three pillars are ecological, social and … (one word)
ACTIVATION-ANSWER = economic
ACTIVATION-HINT = It is the dimension of jobs, companies and budgets.
ACTIVATION-FEEDBACK = Ecological, social and economic. Ask yourself which of these gets the most attention in your own institution.

SLIDE-TITLE = Model 2: The Nested Model
NARRATION-0 = The nested model, sometimes called the Russian-doll model, draws the same three dimensions differently. Nothing can happen outside the environment.
IMAGE-ALT = Nested circles showing the economy inside society and society inside the environment
IMAGE-PATH = img/1280px-Nested_sustainability-v2.svg.png
IMAGE-CAPTION = Nested sustainability-v2 – KTucker – CC BY-SA 3.0 – via Wikimedia Commons
INTRO-TEXT = """
The **economy sits inside society**, and **society sits inside the environment**. The economy depends on society, and both depend on the environment.

This is the counter-image to equal pillars: the environment sets the frame. It links to the idea of **strong sustainability** from Session 2_1: the ecological dimension is not exchangeable.
"""
NARRATION-1 = The picture is about dependence.
KEY-MESSAGE = The nested model is a picture of dependence, so trade-offs are limited: the environment does not sit next to the others as an equal partner.

ACTIVATION-ANSWER = society
ACTIVATION-HINT = Economy inside this, and this inside the environment.
ACTIVATION-FEEDBACK = The economy sits inside society, and society sits inside the environment.

SLIDE-TITLE = Pillars or Doughnut?
NARRATION-0 = Milica compares the two models that matter most for her board. The nested model sits in between: its trade-offs are limited, because the environment sets the frame.
CONCEPT-A = Three pillars
CONCEPT-B = Doughnut
CRITERION-1 = Picture
A-1 = three equal pillars or overlapping circles
B-1 = a ring between a floor and a ceiling
CRITERION-2 = Trade-offs
A-2 = possible ("balance")
B-2 = not allowed: two hard limits
CRITERION-3 = Fits
A-3 = weak sustainability
B-3 = strong sustainability and social justice
CRITERION-4 = Good for
A-4 = starting a conversation
B-4 = decisions involving limits
NARRATION-1 = Here is how each model frames the coal region.
EXAMPLE-A = Jobs in coal regions (social, economic) can be "balanced" against emissions (ecological).
EXAMPLE-B = Decent jobs for coal workers and the climate are both hard limits. The question becomes *how* to move into the safe space: a just transition, new green skills.
NARRATION-2 = Here is a common error.
MISCONCEPTION = "The nested model shows that the economy is the most important part." It shows the opposite: the economy depends on society and on the environment.
NARRATION-3 = Now you decide. Which model does each feature belong to?
STATEMENT-1 = Allows balancing between dimensions
STATEMENT-2 = Going below the inner ring counts as failure
STATEMENT-3 = Easy to use for starting a conversation
STATEMENT-4 = Economic activity is a means of staying inside the safe space
CLASSIFY-HINT = Ask for each model: what does its picture forbid?
CLASSIFY-FEEDBACK = The pillars allow trade-offs, which is also the main critique. The doughnut sets two hard limits. Many practitioners use the pillars to start a conversation and the doughnut to guide decisions.
