[Content_Introduction_Template_2.md]
SLIDE-TITLE = A Critical Look
NARRATION-0 = The sceptical teacher has a point. The SDGs are a strong framework, but they are not perfect. Juliana Hilf always presents the critique alongside the goals, so that you can use them with open eyes.
GUIDING-QUESTION = What should you keep in mind when you work with the SDGs?
NARRATION-1 = First, goals can conflict.
ASPECT-1 = **Conflicts between goals:** for example SDG 8 (economic growth) versus SDG 13 (climate action)
NARRATION-2 = Second, they are hard to measure.
ASPECT-2 = **Too broad and vague:** progress is hard to measure, with data gaps and inconsistent monitoring
NARRATION-3 = Third, they may ask too much and look at too little.
ASPECT-3 = **Ambition and blind spots:** overly ambitious for countries that lack infrastructure and funding; structural causes of inequality not sufficiently addressed; Western development models
NARRATION-4 = And fourth, growth and greenwashing. Greenwashing is when companies look greener than they are.
ASPECT-4 = **Growth and greenwashing:** the growth paradigm remains unchallenged; corporate influence, greenwashing (companies looking greener than they are) and symbolic politics
NARRATION-5 = Here is what follows.
WHY-IT-MATTERS = Strong political will and good governance matter. None of the 17 goals is on track (2026), so work with the framework where you can, and be honest about what it cannot deliver.

NARRATION-Q-0 = First, help Stefan. Write your answer, then compare.
QUESTION-1 = You review the curriculum for "electrician". Which SDG targets would you map it against, and how?
MODEL-ANSWER-1 = """
Start with **SDG 4.4** (skills for employment, decent jobs and entrepreneurship) and **SDG 4.7** (skills for sustainable development): an electrician curriculum that includes solar systems and energy efficiency directly serves both. A **ministry officer** would add the system level: does the national VET strategy name these targets, and are they funded? A **principal** would ask: do the teachers have the green competences, and do partners offer training places in green technologies? The method is the point: pick the occupation, ask which goals its work advances, and write that down.
"""
NARRATION-Q-1 = Now for a conflict, the kind the sceptical teacher was talking about.
QUESTION-2 = Which SDG conflict do you see in your region, for example SDG 8 versus SDG 13? Who is affected, and what could a realistic compromise look like?
MODEL-ANSWER-2 = """
A **ministry officer** would usually name the tension between growth targets and climate targets, for example when a region depends on energy-intensive industry. A **school developer** might see it in curricula: training for current jobs versus adding green competences. A **principal** experiences it in the budget: energy-saving investments cost money the school may not have. A realistic compromise is rarely "choose one goal". It is sequencing: which targets come first, which skills prepare learners for both, and where funding can follow.
"""
NARRATION-Q-2 = Finally, your own programme.
QUESTION-3 = Choose one VET programme in Serbia and link two SDGs or targets to it. Why is the link convincing?
MODEL-ANSWER-3 = """
The work of revising programmes, supporting schools and steering the system is already a documented contribution to the national SDG agenda. You do not need a new project to "start with the SDGs". You need to **name the goals your work already serves** and to **show them** in strategy documents, curricula and quality reports. A convincing link names the goal, the target and one concrete element of the programme.
"""
