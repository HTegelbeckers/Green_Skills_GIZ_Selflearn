[Concept_Explanation_Template_2.md]
SLIDE-TITLE = Three Responses to Limits
NARRATION-0 = The responses to ecological limits can be grouped in three: efficiency, sufficiency and degrowth strategies. Milica compares the first two, because she can act on them at school tomorrow.
CONCEPT-A = Efficiency
CONCEPT-B = Sufficiency
CRITERION-1 = Means
A-1 = the same result with less input: less energy, less material, less waste
B-1 = use less in total
CRITERION-2 = Question
A-2 = How cheap can we make it?
B-2 = Do we need it at all?
CRITERION-3 = Fits
A-3 = weak sustainability
B-3 = is often discussed together with efficiency
CRITERION-4 = Risk
A-4 = rebound effect: if it gets cheaper, people may use more, and the saving disappears
B-4 = needs people to change habits
NARRATION-1 = Here are examples of each.
EXAMPLE-A = Replacing old lamps with LED lamps, or fitting a more efficient motor into a machine
EXAMPLE-B = Taking fewer car trips, or repairing a device instead of buying a new one
NARRATION-2 = The third response is the most misunderstood.
MISCONCEPTION = "Degrowth means no economy at all." **Degrowth strategies** (strong sustainability) mean planning an economy that works within ecological limits, with less total growth, and not just a greener version of today's economy.
NARRATION-3 = Now you sort them.
STATEMENT-1 = Replacing old lamps with more efficient LED lamps
STATEMENT-2 = Taking fewer car trips because you already have what you need
STATEMENT-3 = Fitting a more efficient motor into a machine
STATEMENT-4 = Repairing a device instead of buying a new one
CLASSIFY-HINT = Efficiency = the same result with less input. Sufficiency = less use in total.
CLASSIFY-FEEDBACK = LED lamps and a more efficient motor give the same result with less input. Fewer car trips and repairing mean less use in total. Note that repairing also has an efficiency side.
