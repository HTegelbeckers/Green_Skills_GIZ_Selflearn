## A Report from 1972

    --{{0}}--
In 1972, a team at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology, including Donella Meadows and Dennis Meadows, wrote a report for the Club of Rome. In this video, Dennis Meadows explains how the study came about.

> [!TIP] Watch for
> 1. who commissioned the report
> 2. what the team wanted to show about the relationship between growth and the planet

!?[The Origin of Limits to Growth: Interview with Dennis Meadows](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U7Z6h-U4CmI "VolkswagenStiftung – The Origin of 'Limits to Growth' – Interview with Dennis Meadows (6:02)")

<details>

<summary>Text summary of the video</summary>

In 1972, a team at MIT (Donella Meadows, Dennis Meadows and colleagues) wrote *The Limits to Growth* for the **Club of Rome**, a group of thinkers, scientists and business people that commissions studies on global problems. Its co-founder Aurelio Peccei explained the club's purpose in an [archive film](https://www.open.ac.uk/library/digital-archive/video:00521_2519).

The report used a computer model called **World3**, in the tradition of Jay Forrester's **system dynamics**: a way of modelling how parts of a system influence each other over time. World3 simulated how population, industrial production, food, resources and pollution interact.

</details>

## Three Responses to Limits

    --{{0}}--
The responses to ecological limits can be grouped in three: efficiency, sufficiency and degrowth strategies. Milica compares the first two, because she can act on them at school tomorrow.

<!-- data-type="none" -->
| Criterion | Efficiency | Sufficiency |
|-----------|---------------|---------------|
| Means | the same result with less input: less energy, less material, less waste | use less in total |
| Question | How cheap can we make it? | Do we need it at all? |
| Fits | weak sustainability | is often discussed together with efficiency |
| Risk | rebound effect: if it gets cheaper, people may use more, and the saving disappears | needs people to change habits |

    --{{1}}--
Here are examples of each.

      {{1}}
- **Efficiency:** Replacing old lamps with LED lamps, or fitting a more efficient motor into a machine
- **Sufficiency:** Taking fewer car trips, or repairing a device instead of buying a new one

    --{{2}}--
The third response is the most misunderstood.

      {{2}}
> [!WARNING] Common misconception
> "Degrowth means no economy at all." **Degrowth strategies** (strong sustainability) mean planning an economy that works within ecological limits, with less total growth, and not just a greener version of today's economy.

<details>

<summary>✅ Model answer</summary>

**Plan the next five years for both:** support the transition of existing mining programmes, and build programmes that can grow within limits, such as renewables installation, efficiency or repair. A sector built on a finite resource will shrink, but that does not mean the region has no future. Keeping everything as before ignores the limit; closing the school immediately ignores the skills and jobs the region still needs.
